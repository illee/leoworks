How do I check whether my brand shows up in Naver's AI Briefing (Korea's AI search answer)?

Short answer: run your keywords through Naver search logged out and read the AI answer at the top — or let a scheduled tool do it every day. Naver AI Briefing Monitor checks each keyword, tells you whether Naver showed an AI briefing, and returns the answer, the sources it cites, any ads inside it, and whether your brand and your competitors are mentioned or recommended. It needs no Naver login and costs $3 per 1,000 queries checked, plus $12 per 1,000 briefings captured.

Naver is Korea's #1 search engine, and its AI briefing (AI 브리핑) is the generated answer at the top of the results page — Naver's version of Google AI Overviews. If you sell in Korea, it is the first answer many shoppers read, so it is worth knowing what it says about your category.

What the tool reads

For each query you give it, the tool loads Naver search (PC by default, mobile optional) and records:

  • Whether a briefing appeared (hasBriefing). Not every query gets one. In a test of 20 queries on 2026-10-02, 8 of 20 showed a briefing — mostly informational, how-to, review and comparison queries, rarely bare "OO 추천" (OO recommendations) or brand-name-only queries.
  • The answer text and the short highlighted answer when there is one.
  • Cited sources — title, URL and platform (Naver Blog, Naver Clip, news sites, other websites).
  • Ads Naver put inside the briefing, if any.
  • Brand judgments — for each brand and competitor you list: mentioned or not, recommended or not, sentiment, and how many cited sources name it.

How to set it up (5 minutes)

  1. Open the tool on Apify: Naver AI Briefing Monitor.
  2. Add queries — the keywords your buyers search. Informational, how-to, review and comparison queries get briefings more often than bare "recommendation" or brand-name-only queries:
    {
  "queries": ["무선청소기 추천 2026", "콜라겐 효과"],
  "brandNames": ["다이슨"],
  "competitorNames": ["삼성", "LG"]
}
    Use brand names (삼성, LG) rather than product-line names — the AI answer usually names the brand. Up to 10 brands and competitors per run.
  3. Run it once to see which of your queries get a briefing at all. The form default (two queries, no brands) costs about $0.03.
  4. Schedule it daily. Answers and cited sources change over time; a daily run turns the dataset into a history of who Naver's AI mentions.
  5. Read the results as a table in Apify, download them, or pull them by API.

For scale: 20 queries checked daily for 30 days, with about 40% of them showing a briefing, is $4.68. Queries that fail to load are reported with an error field and not charged.

If the briefing cites Naver Blog posts — as in the vacuum example — Naver Blog Brand Monitor can tell you which of those posts are sponsored.

You can also call the tool from Claude, Cursor or Claude Code through the Apify MCP server — ask "Check whether 다이슨 is mentioned in Naver's AI answer for 무선청소기 추천 2026 and list the cited sources" and the agent runs it. Setup is on the tool page.

How accurate is it?

Briefing presence, answer and cited sources matched what a browser showed for 20 test queries. The mention / recommendation judgments agreed with hand labels on 97% of 38 judgments. That is a small test, and answers can differ between sessions — read the trend over days, not one check.

Other ways, and their limits

  • Search it yourself. Fine for a handful of keywords. It doesn't scale to daily checks across a category, and a logged-in browser can show you a different page than a new visitor sees.
  • Screenshot and compare by hand. Works for a one-off report, but you lose the structure — which source was cited, which brand was recommended — that you need to compare week to week.
  • Track only your classic rankings. Useful, but the AI answer sits above them and cites its own sources — in the vacuum example above, four of five were blog posts.
